Principles

Four things that will stay true.

Readers and commenters are never seats.

Viewers and commenters are free at every tier, in any number. Feedback is the point of the product. We won’t tax it.

Billing never breaks a published URL.

Going over a limit or lapsing a plan makes a workspace read-only. Nothing is deleted, and no link you’ve shared goes dark.

Agents don’t consume seats.

Your agents publish through your workspace. We charge for the people who hold publishing seats, not for the bots that draft for them.

Self-hosting is free, forever.

The whole product is Fair Source and runs as one container. The hosted tier pays for the service, not the software.

Questions

The fine print, said plainly.

What counts as an editor?

Anyone who publishes work during a billing period. Viewers and commenters are never counted, no matter how many. Guests can suggest changes in comments for free: a guest only becomes a billable editor when a workspace owner explicitly grants them an editor seat. For example, 10 people read a workspace, 2 leave comments, and 1 publishes: you pay for 1 editor.

What happens if we stop paying?

Your workspace becomes read-only above the free limits. Every artifact stays live at its URL, with its full version history and comments. Publishing resumes when the plan does.

Can we self-host instead?

Yes. One container is the whole product: SQLite and local disk by default, Postgres and S3 at scale. The hosted tier exists for teams who would rather not run it themselves.

What is Derive's license?

Derive is fair source (FSL-1.1-ALv2): use, modify, and self-host it freely for anything except selling Derive itself as a service. Every release converts to Apache-2.0 two years after it ships.

Do we need a card to start?

No. The Free plan needs no card and has no time limit. When your team outgrows it, upgrade in the app; your artifacts and links are never affected.
