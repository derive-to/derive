The code is public and you can run the complete product on your own infrastructure. The

## The restriction

You cannot offer Derive as a competing commercial product or service. For example, you cannot
take this code and sell a hosted Derive service.

Internal use, consulting work, and running Derive for your own organization are allowed. Email
hello@derive.to if you are unsure whether a planned use is allowed.

## Conversion to Apache 2.0

Each release converts to the [Apache License 2.0](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0)
two years after its release date. This conversion is part of the license and does not depend on
Derive Labs continuing to operate. After conversion, the restriction on competing commercial use
no longer applies to that release.

## Is Derive open source?

Not under the Open Source Initiative definition. The FSL is not OSI-approved because it limits
competing commercial use for two years. We describe Derive as Fair Source instead.

## Why use the FSL?

The FSL lets people inspect, modify, and self-host Derive while reserving competing commercial
hosting for a limited period. The scheduled Apache 2.0 conversion makes the long-term grant clear
from the day each release ships.

We chose it over the AGPL because the FSL does not add copyleft obligations to internal use. For a
longer comparison, read [Armin Ronacher's FSL and AGPL article](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2024/9/23/fsl-agpl-open-source-businesses/).
